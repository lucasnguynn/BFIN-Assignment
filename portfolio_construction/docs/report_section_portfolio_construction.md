# Equity allocation, robustness and evaluation design

## Why this allocation?

Kappa's fixed commitments come first. We use 2,400 Beta zero-coupon bonds and 3,600 Delta coupon bonds to support the $2.4 million payment in 2024 and $3.6 million in 2027. This uses $5,764,524 of the initial $12 million. The $6,235,476 balance is the growth budget, including $6,229.25 in assumed entry fees. Investing that growth budget cannot be mistaken for funding the two fixed liabilities. The initial cash buffer is zero; the annual $126,000 Delta coupons build a separate cash balance. At 0% cash interest the coupons total $630,000 after 2027. We should explicitly tell the committee that zero initial liquidity is a choice with downside consequences.

Our candidate universe has ten US common stocks in six sectors and two US equity ETFs: AAPL, MSFT, JPM, BAC, JNJ, PFE, PG, KO, XOM, CAT, VTV and IWM. The first ETF covers US large-cap value; the second covers US small-cap stocks. Both are part of our research universe even if the optimizer allocates them zero weight. VTI is a separate investable benchmark. The source-date register documents case-date eligibility; however, the team should still retain copies of any original filings it relies upon.

## Return forecast and risk estimation

We use 96 common monthly Yahoo Finance adjusted-price returns, January 2015 to December 2022. The October 2026 retrieval was retrospective, so adjustments and survivor selection are sources of look-ahead and revision risk. Forecasting each stock's annual arithmetic return from its own full-sample average would overstate the information we possess. We instead put 25% weight on each stock's monthly historical mean and 75% on VTI's monthly historical mean, then multiply by 12. The 25% weight is a transparent judgment rather than an empirically optimal shrinkage estimate.

The annual sample covariance is 12 times the monthly sample covariance. For the alternative single-index estimate, we regress each candidate asset's monthly returns against VTI, then combine common-market covariance with asset-specific residual variances and assume cross-residual covariance is zero. The latter assumption is not innocuous: in our data, JPM and BAC have a residual correlation of about 0.795; PG and KO have about 0.527. Consequently, we use single-index covariance as a model challenge, not a demonstration of superior diversification.

Foundation risk aversion is set at A=3. Our growth optimization uses the foundation allocation scaling factor f = $6,229,246.75 / $12 million = 0.519104 under a fixed funding-sleeve approximation. This means that the effective coefficient in a growth-only formulation is A times f, not the unscaled number A. We also rerun the same model at foundation A=6. This conditional objective excludes the market-value covariance of the case bonds with equities and changes in the foundation's capital base after mandatory withdrawals. We therefore do not call the resulting figures complete fund utility.

## Portfolio decision and inconvenient findings

We impose long-only weights summing to 100%, with at most 15% in any single stock, 30% across direct stocks in one sector, 30% in any ETF and 40% in both ETFs together. The 30% sector cap does not look through ETF underlying holdings. Under common constraints the selected Sample Mean-Variance portfolio has an estimated annual arithmetic return of 12.05%, annual volatility of 14.98% and an equity contribution to foundation utility of 0.053469. Single-Index Mean-Variance gives 11.91%, 14.46% and 0.053348 on the sample covariance measure. Constrained Minimum Variance gives 10.86%, 13.15% and 0.049374. We select the Sample MV solution because its estimated utility is highest under our baseline inputs, rather than because its realized performance is demonstrably better.

The weaker evidence is worth reporting. Minimum Variance has lower modeled volatility. Sample MV's margin over Single-Index MV is small. At foundation A=6, the reoptimized Sample MV portfolio has expected return 11.85% and annual volatility 14.30%, which demonstrates that preference assumptions matter. If we hold the original Sample MV and Minimum-Variance weights fixed and reduce the stock-specific historical mean weight from 0.25 to about 0.0842, Minimum Variance becomes preferable under foundation A=3. Alternatively, with historical mean weight held at 0.25, the fixed-weight utility ranking reverses around foundation A=8.91. Neither number is the threshold for portfolios that are reoptimized after assumptions change.

Concentration has been deliberately tightened from 20% stock/35% sector to 15%/30% at a small baseline utility cost, which is a governance preference, not a mathematical dominance claim. Bootstrapped resamples show highly unstable optimal weights: MSFT reaches the 15% cap in roughly 95% of our 120 moving-block samples, and AAPL in roughly 82.5%. A retrospective test that estimates weights on 2015-2019 and applies them to 2020-2022 produces about 13.66% CAGR and -21.71% maximum drawdown for Sample MV, versus 10.67% CAGR and -18.87% drawdown for Minimum Variance. This is a diagnostic, not genuinely unseen out-of-sample evidence, because the research universe was selected retrospectively.

## Trustee risk, long-term goal and what we would monitor

We interpret a 15% rolling 12-month withdrawal-adjusted modeled whole-foundation investment loss as a compulsory committee review trigger; 12% is an early warning. Mandatory withdrawals must not be classified as losses. The bonds have no observed post-2022 resale prices, so we cannot claim to have measured realized total-fund bond mark-to-market returns. Contractual funding coverage remains a distinct risk control. Under an instantaneous hypothetical 30% uniform equity decline and 100 bp increase in both Beta and Delta yields, the initial fund loses about 17.27%. This is a genuine weakness of the chosen policy. It is neither a measured twelve-month result nor a model-implied maximum loss.

Assuming 2.3% annual inflation, the 2030 purchasing-power target is about $9.596 million nominal. With the retained Delta coupons held at 0%, the growth budget needs about a 4.66% annual compounded return to reach the target. Our estimated arithmetic return of 12.05% is not a forecast of CAGR, a guarantee of goal achievement or the basis of a 2030 success probability without an explicit return-distribution model.

## What Member 2 will do after the original submission

The growth allocation is to be bought using a 3 January 2023 closing-price proxy and a one-off 10 bp transaction cost. No elective rebalance is allowed. We make no discretionary trades or target-weight rebalancing. Dividends are treated as reinvested in the paying security using adjusted-close total-return proxies; the economic share count may increase as weights drift. We do not elect voluntary exchanges or tender offers, and we will audit the vendor treatment of corporate actions during the post-freeze evaluation rather than assume that an adjusted close fully resolves them. The VTI comparison uses exactly the same entry date and cost. After the genuine 22 October freeze and an archived 2023-2025 retrieval, we will report growth-only realized compound return, volatility, maximum drawdown, tracking error and active returns. This does not estimate full-fund 2030 mission success. The response to the trustees' new mandate belongs in a separately dated change log after 23 October and cannot revise the original policy retrospectively.
