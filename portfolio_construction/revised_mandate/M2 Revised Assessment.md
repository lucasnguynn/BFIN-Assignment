# Member 2 | Equity decision under the revised Kappa mandate

**Committee memo text — analysis for group approval, 24 October 2026.** All original and revised policy comparisons are valued as at 31 December 2022. Observed 2023–2025 market returns are reported afterwards as a test of the frozen strategy, never as an input to its original optimization.

## Recommendation and funding trade-off

Moving USD 1.2 million of the learning-centre payment from 2027 to 2025 does not increase Kappa's USD 6 million nominal commitments, but it changes how much capital can be left in equities. The original Beta/Delta funding cost USD 5,764,524 and left USD 6,229,246.75 of net equity investments after a 10 bp entry fee. Member 1's revised principal-matching plan costs USD 5,784,228, leaving USD 6,209,562.44 in equities; its retained 2030 coupon cash is USD 564,000. Member 1's alternative coupon-financed plan costs USD 5,429,776.18 and frees USD 6,563,660.16 for equities, but leaves only USD 168,000 retained cash in 2030.

**M2's provisional policy recommendation** is to retain the frozen Sample Mean-Variance (MV) equity weights and use revised principal matching as the conservative funding reference, pending the trustees' choice. The coupon alternative provides approximately USD 354,098 more initial equity capital and a lower modeled growth hurdle, but it has essentially **no cash slack immediately after the 2025 obligation**, compared with roughly USD 396,000 under principal matching. For trustees concerned about losses near 15%, the latter distinction is material. A USD 250,000 unspent operating buffer alongside coupon funding would leave approximately USD 6.314 million net equities and raise its modeled 2030 equity CAGR hurdle to about 4.787%; this is a separately labeled buffer sensitivity, not the selected zero-buffer case.

## Equity weights, rejected alternative and model risk

The original optimization used 96 common monthly observations from January 2015 through December 2022, a 25% own-history / 75% VTI-prior mean forecast, long-only positions, a 15% cap per direct stock and a 30% cap per direct-stock sector. Sample MV at foundation risk aversion A=3 is the frozen proposal. Minimum Variance is a credible rejected risk-focused alternative; Single-Index MV and A=6 test model and preference sensitivity. The frozen Sample MV portfolio owns eight directly held stocks and allocates zero to its two ETF candidates. That is permitted by the case, but it creates concentrated positions at or near caps; we should not describe it as broadly diversified simply because the candidate universe contains two ETFs.

Changing the funding budget alone does not change the normalized weights. When reoptimization is allowed, the sample-covariance equity-utility contribution under revised principal funding moves only from **0.053328705 to 0.053328718**; under revised coupon funding, from **0.055826785 to 0.055830594**. These tiny gains do not by themselves justify replacing the frozen weights. The revised fixed-weight Sample MV versus Minimum Variance switch occurs at approximately **A=8.94** for principal matching or **A=8.46** for coupon funding. These are pairwise fixed-policy thresholds, not switching points for fully reoptimized strategies. The utility values measure an equity-sleeve contribution with the bond allocation held fixed, **not a complete dynamic whole-foundation utility**.

To prevent attributing every change to the trustees, the supporting output `mandate_effect_bridge.csv` distinguishes (i) changed payment timing under principal matching and frozen equity weights, (ii) the optional change in bond-funding technique, and (iii) optional equity reoptimization. Under an instantaneous equity −25% / bond-yield +100 bp stress, initial-fund losses are approximately **14.42%** (principal) and **15.08%** (coupon). This is not the board's rolling 12-month, withdrawal-adjusted 12% warning / 15% review measure, and neither threshold is a guaranteed loss cap.

## Factors and the evidence against the original choice

Member 3's 2015–2022 French-factor regressions use the same 96 monthly observations and static-weight return proxy. The frozen portfolio's adjusted R² rises from **0.785 for CAPM** to **0.844 for FF3**; FF3 market beta is approximately **0.867**, SMB exposure **−0.426** and HML exposure about **0.010**. Estimated FF3 alpha is positive at **0.00569 per month** in that historical specification. This is evidence of an in-sample residual, not proof of a persistent, investable abnormal return: forecast estimation, retrospective stock selection and revised vendor prices limit that claim. A funding-only scenario using identical normalized weights should keep the same historical factor exposures. Reoptimized weights need their own factor re-estimation by Member 3.

The separate observed, price-based 2023–2025 evaluation challenges the decision. Using the original eight-stock buy-and-hold quantities (weights drift), a 3 January 2023 close proxy, and the same 10 bp initial fee for the investable VTI comparator, the frozen equity sleeve generated a **17.6864% after-fee CAGR**, compared with **22.4469% for VTI**. The CAGR shortfall is **4.7605 percentage points**; arithmetic annualized active return was **−4.1069%** and tracking error **6.9062%**. Its annualized monthly-return volatility was **11.8036%** versus **12.6028%** for VTI, while monthly NAV maximum drawdown was **−8.7010%** versus **−9.1098%**. Thus lower measured risk did not compensate for the benchmark return shortfall in this period. The evaluation concerns only the traded equity sleeve, not the fictional bonds or the success of the 2030 real-wealth goal. These observed returns must not be used retroactively to change the 2022 policy.

## Corporate actions, evidence status and monitoring

The August 2023 JNJ/Kenvue transaction was an elective split-off. The frozen no-voluntary-exchange rule requires keeping JNJ and **not** crediting Kenvue shares. In the archived Yahoo source, there is no JNJ 2023 stock split, and the adjusted-to-unadjusted price ratio remains stable over 18–24 August. Independently replacing JNJ's adjusted-close multiplier with ordinary Close-plus-Dividends shareholder returns, without a KVUE allocation, changes the total growth portfolio's 2025 terminal value by just **−USD 188.33**. This supports the no-tender interpretation, but the reconstructed ex-date dividend convention is a numerical sensitivity, not a precise claim about executable pay-date reinvestment.

The 2023–2025 source archive consists of nine yfinance raw response frames, 752 common trading dates and 36 monthly evaluations, retrieved after a documented **22 October 2026 university submission email**. The email record does **not** establish instructor/LMS acceptance, and the exact bytes of the emailed ZIP members have not been independently SHA-verified. Therefore the empirical comparison is **conditional on the original-submission evidence**, even though the underlying prices and numerical results are genuine and source-hashed. JNJ human review, final M1 funding selection and M3 sign-off remain open. For ongoing governance, apply the documented withdrawal-adjusted rolling 12-month fund-loss monitoring rule; report rate/equity shocks separately.
