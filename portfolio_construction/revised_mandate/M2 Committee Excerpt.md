The revised commitment brings USD 1.2 million of the 2027 learning-centre payment forward to 2025. This does not alter total liabilities, but it changes the trade-off between cash-flow certainty and the growth allocation. Under Member 1's principal-matching design, bonds cost USD 5.784 million, leaving USD 6.210 million of net equities after the 10 bp entry cost. Its cash ledger retains roughly USD 396,000 just after the 2025 payment. Coupon funding costs USD 5.430 million and initially invests USD 6.564 million in equities, but the corresponding 2025 cash cushion is essentially zero. Both meet the stated contractual payments under the modeled zero-reinvestment convention; neither removes market risk from the remaining fund.

Our *provisional investment analysis* favors keeping the original Sample Mean-Variance equity weights and treats principal matching as the conservative revised funding reference. The 2015–2022 estimate uses 96 monthly returns, 25% stock-specific/75% VTI-prior expected returns, 15% individual-stock and 30% direct-sector caps, and foundation risk aversion A=3. Reoptimization under revised principal matching increases the model's equity-utility contribution only from 0.053328705 to 0.053328718. The credible Minimum Variance alternative sacrifices forecast return for lower modeled risk; the fixed-weight preference would switch at approximately A=8.94. These results are *not* a comprehensive optimization of both the bond and equity sleeves. The frozen selection holds eight directly owned stocks but no ETFs, a concentration that warrants continued scrutiny.

At 2.3% assumed inflation, the equity CAGR needed to reach the 2030 real purchasing-power objective is 4.795% under principal matching versus 4.631% under coupon funding. An instantaneous equity −25% plus bond-yield +100 bp stress produces approximately 14.42% and 15.08% initial-fund losses, respectively. Those estimates are separate from the foundation's rolling, withdrawal-adjusted 12% warning and 15% review measures; they are not guaranteed maximum losses.

The frozen 2023–2025 growth-only evaluation provides inconvenient evidence. Annualized active return was −4.107%, tracking error 6.906%, and annualized monthly-return volatility 11.804% against VTI's 12.603%. The original strategy earned a positive absolute return but did not beat the simpler investable comparator. This result may inform the future review process; it cannot be inserted into a retrospective choice at the 2022 case date. Similarly, Member 3's stronger FF3 in-sample fit (adjusted R² 0.844 versus CAPM 0.785) cannot establish persistent future alpha.
